# Germany (DE-LU) market adapter

Germany is the third market in the schedule-based framework (Pyomo + HiGHS, 15-minute grid). The adapter
(`euroflex_bess_lab.markets.adapters.germany`, market id `germany`) covers day-ahead energy, symmetric FCR
capacity and asymmetric aFRR capacity. It deliberately rejects the imbalance workflow. The real-data results
are in [reports/germany.md](https://github.com/bozliu/euroflex-bess-lab/blob/main/reports/germany.md), regenerated by `scripts/research/germany_backtest.py`.

| Workflow | Status | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `da_only` | supported (experimental tier) | DE-LU day-ahead, hourly MTU before 2025-10-01 expanded to quarter-hours |
| `da_plus_fcr` | supported (experimental tier) | FCR capacity, 4-hour products, pay-as-cleared |
| `da_plus_afrr` | supported (experimental tier) | aFRR capacity only (`afrr.settlement_mode: capacity_only`) |
| `schedule_revision` | supported wrapper | over `da_only`, `da_plus_fcr`, `da_plus_afrr`; revised energy is valued at day-ahead prices |
| `da_plus_imbalance` | **rejected** | no deliberate imbalance positions in Germany (see below) |

Example configs (synthetic reserve prices, real day-ahead prices):

- `examples/configs/basic/germany_da_only_base.yaml`
- `examples/configs/basic/germany_da_plus_fcr_base.yaml`
- `examples/configs/basic/germany_da_plus_afrr_base.yaml`

```bash
euroflex validate-config examples/configs/basic/germany_da_plus_fcr_base.yaml
euroflex validate-data examples/configs/basic/germany_da_plus_fcr_base.yaml
euroflex backtest examples/configs/basic/germany_da_plus_fcr_base.yaml
```

The examples deliver 2025-10-01, the first SDAC day with 15-minute products. The persistence forecast comes
from 2025-09-30, the last hourly day, so they exercise the hourly-to-15-minute transition.

## Market model

### Day-ahead (EPEX SPOT / SDAC, DE-LU)

- Bidding zone DE-LU (`10Y1001A1001A82H`), local time `Europe/Berlin`, gate closure D-1 12:00 CET. The adapter
  rejects configs with `day_ahead_gate_closure_local` later than 12:00.
- SDAC moved to a 15-minute market time unit on trading day 30 September 2025, for delivery day 1 October 2025
  ([EPEX SPOT](https://www.epexspot.com/en/15-minute-products-market-coupling),
  [NEMO Committee](https://nemo-committee.eu/assets/files/15-minute-mtu-in-sdac-was-implemented.pdf)). Before
  that, DE-LU day-ahead products were hourly.
- Normalisation (`normalize_day_ahead_frame`): the source resolution is read from `resolution_minutes`/`resolution`
  or inferred per local delivery day. Hourly rows become four quarter-hours carrying the hourly price, tagged
  `provenance=expanded_from_60m` and `source_resolution_minutes=60`. Native rows are tagged `source_15m`. This
  follows the ENTSO-E normaliser. Gaps inside a day and missing prices raise errors instead of being filled.
  Days whose source resolution contradicts the SDAC calendar are listed in the series metadata
  (`sdac_mtu_mismatch_days`). DST days keep their local length (92 or 100 quarter-hours).
- Accepted inputs: the framework CSV/parquet layout (`timestamp_utc`, `timestamp_local`, `resolution` or
  `resolution_minutes`, `price_eur_per_mwh`), the public-data layout (`timestamp_utc`, `resolution_minutes`,
  `price_eur_mwh`, as in `data/public/energy_charts/da_prices_DE-LU.parquet`) and raw Energy-Charts JSON
  (`unix_seconds`, `price`).

### No implicit balancing: why `da_plus_imbalance` is rejected

A German balance responsible party (BRP) must keep its balance group balanced in every quarter-hour
(*Bilanzkreistreue*). Unlike Belgium and the Netherlands, the German framework does not let a BRP run a
deliberate imbalance position to earn the imbalance price. The obligation was codified in § 4(2) StromNZV, which
expired at the end of 2025
([text and status](https://www.buzer.de/gesetz/4919/a68624.htm)). It continues in the Bundesnetzagentur
balance-group framework: the standard balance-group contract
([BK6-23-102](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Beschlusskammern/BK06/BK6_83_Zug_Mess/838_bilanzkreisvertrag/bk_vertrag_node.html))
and the decision to strengthen Bilanzkreistreue (BK6-19-218).

The imbalance price is the *reBAP* (regelzonenübergreifender einheitlicher Bilanzausgleichsenergiepreis). It is
one price per quarter-hour for all German balance groups, computed jointly by the four TSOs
([netztransparenz.de](https://www.netztransparenz.de/de-de/Regelenergie/Ausgleichsenergiepreis/reBAP),
[TransnetBW](https://www.transnetbw.de/de/strommarkt/bilanzierung-und-abrechnung/bilanzkreisabrechnung-und-bilanzkreistreue)).
The adapter documents reBAP and accepts an optional `data.imbalance` input as a reference series, but never
trades it.

The rejection happens in three places, each with the same message naming Bilanzkreistreue:

- config validation, including `schedule_revision` with `revision.base_workflow: da_plus_imbalance`;
- `GermanyMarketAdapter.validate_timing` / `load_actuals`;
- `GermanyMarketAdapter.settlement_engine`.

### FCR (frequency containment reserve)

Source: [regelleistung.net, tendering and bidding](https://www.regelleistung.net/de-de/Anbieter-werden/Ausschreibungs-und-Gebotsverfahren),
[FCR settlement](https://www.regelleistung.net/de-de/Anbieter-werden/Abrechnung/Frequency-Containment-Reserve).

- Joint procurement in the FCR Cooperation with a daily auction closing at D-1 08:00.
- Six 4-hour products per day (00-04, ..., 20-24 local time). Products are symmetric. The minimum bid is 1 MW, in
  1 MW increments.
- Pay-as-cleared: every awarded MW earns the marginal price of the cooperation, or a local German price when an
  import or export limit binds. Activation energy is not remunerated separately.
- Activation is full within 30 s at ±200 mHz, held for up to 15 minutes, with a ±10 mHz dead band.
- regelleistung.net publishes the German FCR settlement price in **EUR/MW per 4-hour product**. The parser divides
  it by the product length (3 or 5 hours on DST days) to get EUR/MW/h. This reproduces published Q1 2026
  figures exactly (see the report).

**Energy rule for limited-energy reservoirs (what is modelled).** The German PQ conditions
([PQ-Bedingungen v1.04, 3 June 2022](https://www.regelleistung.net/Portals/1/downloads/regelenergieanbieter_werden/pr%C3%A4qualifikationsbedingungen/PQ%20Bedingungen%20-%2003.06.2022%20(deutsch).pdf),
section 3.1.6) size a 1 MW symmetric FCR unit at 0.83 MWh. Per direction that is 0.415 MWh:

| Component | MWh |
| --- | ---: |
| Alert state: 15 minutes at full power | 0.25 |
| Maximum of the previous-activation and storage-management-lag allowances | 0.125 |
| 5-minute bridge to reserve mode | 0.04 |

The research LP keeps `soc - 0.415 h x FCR / eta_d >= soc_min` and `soc + 0.415 h x FCR x eta_c <= soc_max` at
both ends of every quarter-hour. SOGL Art. 156(10) allows 15-30 minutes for the alert-state duration. The
Continental Europe regulators referred that decision to ACER in July 2026
([ACER](https://www.acer.europa.eu/news/acer-decide-minimum-activation-period-frequency-containment-reserves-providers)).
The report shows the 30-minute case (0.665 h per direction) as a sensitivity. The engine path uses
`fcr.sustain_duration_minutes: 30`, the nearest framework value (multiples of 15) that is not below 24.9 minutes.

### aFRR (automatic frequency restoration reserve)

Sources: regelleistung.net tendering page (above),
[aFRR activation](https://www.regelleistung.net/de-de/Anbieter-werden/Aktivierung-der-Reserven/automatic-Frequency-Restoration-Reserve),
[ALPACA](https://www.regelleistung.net/en-us/European-cooperations/ALPACA-aFRR-capacity-market),
[PICASSO](https://www.entsoe.eu/network_codes/eb/picasso/).

- **Capacity market (RLM).** Daily auction closing at D-1 09:00. Products are 4-hour blocks with separate
  positive (POS) and negative (NEG) directions. Pricing is **pay-as-bid**: each awarded bid earns its own capacity
  price. Germany procures jointly with Austria and Czechia in ALPACA.
- **Energy market (RAM).** Quarter-hour products closing 25 minutes before delivery, settled pay-as-cleared
  through PICASSO and IGCC. The German TSOs joined PICASSO on 22 June 2022. Every awarded capacity bid must be
  matched by energy bids. Full activation within 5 minutes.
- **Energy rule for limited-energy reservoirs.** PQ-Bedingungen section 2.7.1 requires 60 minutes of energy per
  marketed MW, held for both directions when both are offered. The research LP keeps 1.0 h per direction for the
  whole block; this is conservative, because the rule allows a pro-rata buffer towards the end of the marketing
  window. The engine path uses `afrr.sustain_duration_minutes: 60`. With 60-minute buffers a 2 MWh battery can
  hold about 0.9 MW up and 0.9 MW down at the same time.
- **15-minute capacity products** (TSO project FlexRL) were expected for autumn 2026 but have been postponed
  ([FlexRL](https://www.regelleistung.net/de-de/Marktinformationen/Laufende-Projekte/FlexRL)). The results
  through 2026-10-25 contain 4-hour products only.

**What is modelled: capacity only.**

- The benchmark capacity price is the published German **average awarded price**
  (`GERMANY_AVERAGE_CAPACITY_PRICE`). That is a benchmark for a pay-as-bid auction, not what an individual bid
  earns. The report adds a pay-as-bid sensitivity: the bid is the persistence price (optionally shaded), it is
  accepted only if it does not exceed the realised marginal price, and it earns its own bid.
- Activation energy is **not** modelled, because a battery's activation depends on its energy-bid position in the
  PICASSO merit order, which public data does not identify. Expected activation is zero, while the 60-minute
  energy buffer is reserved.
- In the engine path the config sets `afrr.settlement_mode: capacity_only` and `afrr.activation_mode: none`, and
  omits activation inputs. The adapter then supplies zero activation series (`provenance=zero_activation_capacity_only_assumption`).
  Users with their own activation data can use the expected-value mode with the four activation inputs. CSV
  forecasts must also provide activation forecast files, which should be zeros for capacity-only.

### Decision timing (no look-ahead)

Reserve auctions close before the day-ahead auction, so for `da_plus_fcr` and `da_plus_afrr` the adapter's
`decision_schedule` sets the joint decision time to the reserve gate closure: D-1 08:00 for FCR and D-1 09:00
for aFRR.

- The engine reads that time from `day_ahead_gate_closure_*`. The true day-ahead gate closure is kept in
  `day_ahead_market_gate_closure_*`, and `decision_time_basis` names the rule.
- `da_only` decides at D-1 12:00.
- Persistence uses the previous local day: day-ahead prices for D-1 were published on D-2, and reserve results
  for D-1 were published on D-2, so no data from after the decision time is used.

### Engine path vs. block-constant research LP

The framework solver lets reserve volumes change every quarter-hour. German products are 4-hour blocks, so
`solve_germany_day` co-optimises one delivery day with reserve volumes constant per local 4-hour block. It also
keeps the PQ buffers at both ends of each interval and, as in the framework, uses a binary that forbids
simultaneous charge and discharge.

- In replication mode (per-quarter-hour reserve, start-of-interval check, framework durations and throughput
  degradation) it reproduces the engine objective exactly. This is checked in
  `tests/test_germany_block_solver.py` and in the report's consistency section.
- The block constraint is worth a few percent of reserve value (see the report's sensitivities).
- Engine support for block-constant reserves would need a solver and engine change (a block length threaded from
  the adapter into `optimization/solver.py`); until then the example configs use the relaxation.

The example and test reserve prices are **synthetic illustrative values**, labelled
`synthetic_values_not_regelleistung_net`. The regelleistung.net exports are xlsx files; the adapter reads them
with the standard library (`read_xlsx_rows`, `parse_regelleistung_fcr_results`,
`parse_regelleistung_afrr_results`), so no extra dependency is needed. The research script prefers the
`euroflex_bess_lab.public_data` loaders and falls back to its own cache (`--fetch`). Both parsers agree to
float32 precision.

## Limitations

- Day-ahead only: no continuous intraday or intraday auctions, no re-trading after gate closure.
  `schedule_revision` re-optimises at day-ahead prices as a proxy for intraday re-trading.
- No imbalance revenue by design.
- FCR activation energy and storage-management trades are not modelled. FCR energy is assumed to net to zero per
  block, with the PQ buffer reserved.
- aFRR is capacity-only. The average-price benchmark overstates what a simple pay-as-bid bidder earns; the
  report quantifies this.
- Price taker with perfect acceptance (except in the pay-as-bid sensitivity). Bid granularity (1 MW minimum and
  increments) is assumed to be met by pooling.
- Daily 50 % to 50 % SOC boundary, linear efficiencies, linear degradation, no grid fees or availability losses.
- The framework `PersistenceForecastProvider` needs an equal-length previous local day, so it raises on the day
  after a DST change. The research script then uses the last complete earlier day and lists those days. Engine
  runs across such a day need a different provider.
